The gap regulators, courts, and shareholders are now looking at

AI is moving faster than the governance around it. Most organizations have policies, committees, and technical controls. Few can show, at the board and executive level, who is accountable for what, how decisions get made, and why those decisions were informed. We close that gap.

Every engagement is scoped to one of these, led by Brian Allen, and delivered by senior practitioners assembled for it. Each one states which layer of governance it addresses.

1

Assess

We measure where your oversight program stands today against what regulators, case law, and standards now expect of boards and executives. You get a plain statement of what exists, what is missing, and what matters most.

Why it matters

Mature oversight is how you move faster

Most organizations are putting AI on top of the governance processes they built for cyber: built for protection, slow to approve, focused on what can go wrong. That cannot keep pace with AI. When governance is only compliance and downside, the business routes around it, or it stalls. Moving slowly is a risk too. A competitor that moves faster can take your place.

Tolerances are decision points

A tolerance set by the board tells your people how far they can go without asking. Clear tolerances let decisions happen faster.

Escalation by design

A risk outside tolerance should reach the right person before it becomes an incident, through a defined path rather than through relationships.

Decision-making AI needs this most

As agents begin acting without approval of every step, the question shifts from whether a human touched the decision to whether the process around it was adequate.

The program outlasts the technology

Ad hoc controls turn every new use case into a scramble. A chartered program, built from the oversight layer down, holds steady as the technology changes.

The goal is not the right decision every time. The goal is an informed decision, made through a process you can defend at the scale of the risk.

We call this the Informed Decision Standard. Every engagement is built to meet it.
